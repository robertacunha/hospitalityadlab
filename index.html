Approach

How I build an ad set

The same four steps for any brand. The ads below are what the steps produce once the guest, the levers and the test plan are agreed.

Step 1Learn the guest first

Who books, why and at what price before the first headline. Here that is diners aged 25 to 54 in and around Amsterdam, split by motive: a story worth telling, the craft, or a table that feels personal.

Step 2Pick the lever, then write

Every ad starts from one psychological lever: social currency, effort, scarcity, risk reversal. The copy follows the lever, so each variant is a real test and not a rewording.

Step 3Build the system once

A fixed look and live layouts keep a large set of variants on brand. The restaurant's own photography goes into the designs, so a new season only needs new photos and a new menu line.

Step 4Test one variable at a time

Each round has one variable, one primary read and one guardrail. The winner carries forward and the next idea has to beat it.

The brand

Nine seasonal courses in the Jordaan

NOVE is a seasonal nine-course tasting menu in the Jordaan, Amsterdam. The kitchen spends time with the city's producers first and builds the menu around what it hears. Listen first, cook second. The voice is warm, curious and precise: a good host who never shows off.

Catalogue

Six moments, one evening

A restaurant sells one evening, so the catalogue is six moments of it. Each moment carries four of the twenty-four ads, so no single photo decides the test.

Layouts

One system, four layouts

The brand signals below never change. One layout carries each phase, so the food and the room stay the loudest thing on screen.

LookCandlelight, nothing else

Dark room, warm light, one subject. Hands, plates, glasses and guests, never a wide empty dining room. The wine arc frames the headline the way the room frames the table.

TypeSerif headline, quiet sans

Instrument Serif for headlines and the spaced wordmark, Hanken Grotesk for support lines. Two to seven words in a headline. Sentence case, full stops.

VoiceWarm, curious, precise

A good host who never shows off. Scarcity only where the room is truly small. No invented reviews, no awards, no countdowns.

PlacementsInstagram feed 4:5, Stories and Reels 9:16

Every cell is sized for the placement it runs in. Text stays out of the top 14% and bottom 20% of vertical frames. Placements are set by hand while testing.

Targeting baseLocation first, then interests

Amsterdam plus 25 km, and visitors in the city. Fine dining, wine, chef's table, food travel, seasonal and local food. Lookalikes from bookers once there are 300 or more.

LanguagePlain English, euro pricing

International English for a city full of visitors and expats. Euro prices per guest. Local references stay to one: the Jordaan.

Method

How a round is run

Meta A/B test

Meta splits the audience so cells never overlap. Dynamic creative mixes elements and hides which one won. Use it only after a winner exists, to polish copy.

Seven days minimum per round

Covers the quiet midweek and the weekend rush, which book differently. Stop early only if one cell is losing on cost by 40% or more with enough spend.

Equal budget per cell

Cells share the round budget evenly. A cell needs roughly 25 to 50 of its target event before it is read.

Winners carry forward as messages

The winning idea moves into the next round and new ideas challenge it. Moments rotate between rounds so every photo in the set gets read.

Optimise on a proxy when thin

A twenty-two seat room will not produce 50 reservations a week from ads alone. Early rounds optimise on menu views or booking starts, and the booking tool's own data closes the loop.

Scale with the winners

After round 8, move the winning creative from each phase into one always-on campaign and read cost per cover weekly against the booking system, so no platform grades its own homework.
